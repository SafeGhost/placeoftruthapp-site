The legend

“Place of Truth” was the village of the craftsmen who built the pharaohs’ tombs. From there came a book of dreams, a calendar of good and bad days, and questions to the god.

Three thousand years later the temple opens its doors to you.

Inside the temple

Five rites, one for every day

  1. I

    The vision of your day

    Once a day, rest two fingers on the camera and the Oracle hears your heart. Catch five signs flying out of the dark. Your heart, your signs and your birth star choose a vision meant only for you today.

  2. II

    Ask the Oracle

    About love, family, work, money, your path or yourself. Ask in silence. The answer comes as an image with a sign, a time and advice, and the priest reads it for you.

  3. III

    The pharaohs’ dream book

    Two hundred Egyptian dreams, some taken straight from a papyrus written about 1275 BC, and 789 more from the classic dream book of Gustavus Miller. If a dream was bad, do what the Egyptians did: draw a protective sign and cover it with sand.

  4. IV

    Good and bad days

    Every day of the Egyptian year: good, bad or mixed in the morning, at noon and in the evening, and what the papyrus advises.

  5. V

    Oracle Cards of the 36 stars

    The 36 star groups the Egyptians painted on the ceilings of royal tombs. A card for the day, the star you were born under, and spreads for love and work. Six lessons teach you to read the cards yourself.

The casket of amulets

Twenty-four amulets, each with its story

The temple gives an amulet to those who come back. Each one comes with the story of who wore it and why.

Everyone wore it, from children to pharaohs, against illness and the evil eye. It was put on children and taken on journeys, and painted on the bows of boats so that the boat could "see" its way.
